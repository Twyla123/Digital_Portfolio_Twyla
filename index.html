Research assistantship · USC Economics · Apr 2024 – Jun 2025 · Python

Evaluating Bank Distress from Regulatory and Market Data

Question
Does a bank's funding structure predict distress? Part of the data layer for a study since published as Beyond Hot Money (Dagher and Fuster, 2026).
What I did
Resolved ten U.S. banking datasets that each name a bank differently to one identity per institution, rolled subsidiary financials up the ownership tree, and checked the totals against FDIC data and 10-K filings.
Result
341 of 413 lender IDs matched (72 listed as unresolved rather than forced); 12 years of HMDA records aligned across the 2018 reporting break; a DealScan check that flagged 441 of 28,568 deals (1.54%) for a recording gap that would have overstated time variation.

Research with Prof. Glenn Melnick · USC · 2024 – 2025 · Python and Excel

Revenue Trends in Rural Hospitals, California, 1995–2023

Question
How has the financial position of California's rural hospitals changed over three decades, and what are they living on now?
What I did
Built a 29-year panel (1995–2023) of general acute hospitals from the public HCAI annual financial files; one script rebuilds it and writes every headline figure with the rule that produces it.
Result
Among rural hospitals the outpatient share of gross patient revenue rose from 43% to 68%, the count fell from 79 to 58, and the share classified as Disproportionate Share Hospitals rose from 19% to 48%.

Research · Columbia DiTecT Lab, under Prof. Xuan Sharon Di · Jan – May 2026 · Python

Predicting Survey Response Distributions with LLMs, NYC Congestion Pricing

Question
Surveys are slow and expensive, and they under-cover small groups. Can a language model predict the distribution of answers a demographic group would give, closely enough to be useful before a survey is run?
What I did
Built the inference and fine-tuning pipeline for Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B: answer-option token log-probabilities read in a single forward pass, normalized into a predicted distribution, then LoRA fine-tuned against the empirical distributions with soft-target cross-entropy. Ground truth is the 2022 NYC Citywide Mobility Survey, 23 questions across 17 demographic subgroups of age, gender, income, borough and race. Scored with total variation distance for nominal questions and Wasserstein distance for ordinal ones, against a random-guess baseline and a bootstrapped best-achievable floor.
Result
Across 18 configurations, three prompt formats by three training setups, the best reached 0.241 against 0.308 for a uniform guess on held-out questions. Pre-training on Pew data first made it worse than guessing (0.489 to 0.736), which is the more useful finding: borrowed survey data does not transfer here.

Honors thesis · USC Economics · May 2025 · Python

Wage Polarization and AI: Empirical Evidence from U.S. Labor Market Data

Question
Is AI exposure associated with higher wages, and does the gain reach lower-skill occupations?
What I did
Built a BLS–O*NET occupation panel for 2015–2024 and estimated the association with OLS, quantile regression and difference-in-differences, plus a descriptive look at gender, race and skill level. All sources public: BLS OEWS and CPS, O*NET, BLS CPI-U, Census SOC crosswalks.
Status
Submitted May 2025 and kept as submitted. A 2026 revision with updated data and a revised design is in progress; its results will be posted here with a note on what changed.
